How to use these prompts safely

Every prompt in this pack produces a draft. A draft is not a finding. You are the investigator, you own the output, and the file carries your name. The protocol below is what turns "I used AI" into a documented, reviewable process.

  1. Never paste raw identifying data into a tool your employer has not approved. Use initials or role labels. Complainant becomes CP, respondent becomes RP, witnesses become W1 and W2. Map the labels once in your own notes and keep that map out of the AI tool entirely.
  2. Run the prompt, then edit the output before it touches the file. The AI does not know your policy language, your jurisdiction, your collective agreement or what actually happened in the room. You do.
  3. Log the review. Prompt 30 gives you the entry format. One line per AI-assisted document: what you asked, what you changed, what you rejected, and the date. That log is the artifact that answers the question "what did you do to check it."
  4. Verify every fact the model states back to you. If a draft asserts a date, a policy number, a job title or a quote, trace it to a source document before it survives your edit. Models fill gaps confidently.
  5. Keep opinion out of fact. Several prompts here exist only to enforce that split. Do not let a fluent paragraph blur it back together.
  6. Escalate anything consequential. Protected activity, a criminal allegation, an executive respondent, a threat of violence, or a regulator in the picture. Those go to counsel before you write, not after.

Why the phrasing matters more than the speed

A generic prompt gives you generic language, and generic language in an investigation file is what gets picked apart. "The respondent was unprofessional" is an opinion dressed as a finding. "On 4 March the respondent raised his voice in a team meeting, witnessed by W1 and W2, both of whom described it independently" is a finding.

Every prompt below carries two annotations. Why this phrasing tells you what the wording is doing and why the obvious alternative is worse. What could go wrong tells you the failure mode to watch for when you run it.

The regulatory backdrop, stated plainly and sourced

  • The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) classifies as high risk, in Annex III point 4(b), "AI systems intended to be used to make decisions affecting terms of work-related relationships, the promotion or termination of work-related contractual relationships, to allocate tasks based on individual behaviour or personal traits or characteristics or to monitor and evaluate the performance and behaviour of persons in such relationships." Annex III
  • Article 113 sets the general application date: "It shall apply from 2 August 2026." Article 113
  • Article 26(2) puts the oversight duty on the deployer: "Deployers shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support." Article 26(6) requires deployers to keep the system's automatically generated logs "for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months." Article 26(7) requires employers to inform workers' representatives and affected workers before putting such a system into use. Article 26
  • Article 99(4) sets administrative fines for non-compliance with the deployer obligations in Article 26 at "up to 15 000 000 EUR or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 3 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher." Article 99
  • This pack is not legal advice and it is not a compliance certification. It is a documentation discipline. Whether a given tool in your organisation falls inside Annex III is a question for your counsel, and the answer changes what you have to do. What does not change: a reviewed, logged, human-owned file is better than an unreviewed one in every jurisdiction, including the ones with no AI statute at all.

The thirty prompts

Sequenced by stage, in the order a case actually moves. Open a stage, copy the prompt, replace the bracketed inputs. Read both annotations before you run it.

01

Complaint intake script

Run before the first intake conversation. Produces the questions that capture a complaint completely without leading the complainant or promising an outcome.

Prompt 01
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator preparing an intake conversation. You do not make findings and you do not assess credibility. You produce a draft script the investigator will edit before use.

CONTEXT
- Complaint received via: [CHANNEL, e.g. hotline, manager escalation, direct email]
- Summary as received, in the reporter's own words: [PASTE VERBATIM, USE ROLE LABELS NOT NAMES]
- Policies that may be engaged: [POLICY NAMES]
- Jurisdiction: [COUNTRY / STATE]

TASK
Draft an intake script that captures: what happened, when, where, who was present, whether anything was documented at the time, what the complainant has already told anyone, what outcome they are seeking, and whether they have any safety concern.

CONSTRAINTS
- Open questions only in the first pass. No question may suggest an answer.
- Do not ask why the complainant did not report sooner. Ask what has changed that led them to report now.
- Do not include any question that promises confidentiality, a specific outcome, or a timeline.
- Include one explicit prompt to ask whether the complainant is aware of anyone else affected.
- Include a closing script covering: no retaliation, what happens next in process terms only, and who to contact.
- Flag any question that touches protected characteristics, medical information, or union activity, and mark it "confirm with counsel before asking".

OUTPUT
1. Opening statement to read aloud, under 120 words.
2. Numbered open questions, grouped by topic.
3. Follow-up probes indented under each, to use only if the answer is incomplete.
4. Closing statement to read aloud.
5. A short list of anything you could not draft because the context above did not supply it.
Why this phrasing"What has changed that led you to report now" replaces "why did you wait". The first is a fact question. The second implies the delay undermines the complaint, and it reads that way in a transcript years later.
What could go wrongModels love to draft reassuring language. If a promise of confidentiality survives into your script, you have committed to something no investigation can honour. Strip it.
02

Allegation framing

Turns a narrative complaint into discrete, testable allegations. Everything downstream is scoped by this step, which is why a sloppy pass here costs you weeks later.

Prompt 02
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Convert a narrative complaint into discrete allegations. You do not assess whether any allegation is true.

INPUT
Narrative as received, role labels only: [PASTE]
Applicable policies and their relevant clauses: [PASTE OR SUMMARISE]

TASK
Separate the narrative into individually testable allegations. Each allegation must be capable of being proved or disproved on its own.

RULES FOR EACH ALLEGATION
- State it as: on or about [DATE], [ROLE LABEL] is alleged to have [SPECIFIC OBSERVABLE CONDUCT], in [LOCATION OR CHANNEL].
- Conduct must be observable. Replace conclusions ("harassed", "bullied", "was hostile") with the described behaviour.
- One incident per allegation. Split anything containing "and also" or "repeatedly" into separate numbered items or a defined pattern allegation with its own listed instances.
- Map each allegation to the specific policy clause it would engage if proved. If no policy clause is engaged, say so explicitly rather than forcing a fit.
- Mark each as: SPECIFIC (date, place, conduct all present), PARTIAL (something missing, name what), or VAGUE (not yet testable, name the question that would fix it).

ALSO PRODUCE
- Allegations that appear in the narrative but were not framed as complaints, listed separately.
- Any allegation that, if proved, would engage a legal category rather than only a policy category. Do not analyse the law. Name the category and mark it "counsel review".
- A list of the specific follow-up questions needed to move every PARTIAL and VAGUE item to SPECIFIC.

OUTPUT
A numbered table of allegations with the fields above, then the three lists.
Why this phrasingForcing observable conduct is what stops an investigation from testing a label. You cannot investigate whether someone "was hostile". You can investigate whether they said a specific thing on a specific day.
What could go wrongThe model will invent dates to satisfy the format. Anything not in your source narrative is a hallucination. Mark it PARTIAL and go ask, never let a plausible date stand.
03

Investigator suitability and conflict check

Run on yourself before you accept the case. A conflict found on day one is an administrative matter. The same conflict found at the appeal is a finding overturned.

Prompt 03
You are assisting an HR function deciding who should investigate a complaint. You produce questions and a risk read, not a decision.

CONTEXT
- Nature of the allegations: [SUMMARY, ROLE LABELS ONLY]
- Proposed investigator's role and reporting line: [DESCRIBE]
- Respondent's role and reporting line: [DESCRIBE]
- Complainant's role and reporting line: [DESCRIBE]
- Prior involvement of the proposed investigator with any party: [DESCRIBE OR "NONE KNOWN"]
- Seniority of respondent relative to the investigator: [DESCRIBE]

TASK
Produce a conflict and suitability assessment.

COVER EACH OF THESE
1. Reporting-line conflict, direct or indirect, including dotted lines and shared skip-level.
2. Prior involvement: previous complaints, prior coaching, prior performance decisions, prior friendship or dispute.
3. Perceived conflict, judged from the complainant's point of view and separately from the respondent's point of view. Perception counts even where actual bias is absent.
4. Competence fit: does this case need a specialist, for example allegations involving safety, criminal conduct, financial misconduct, or a protected characteristic.
5. Authority fit: can this investigator realistically compel attendance and access documents given the respondent's seniority.
6. Capacity: does this investigator have the time this case will take.

OUTPUT
- A question list the proposed investigator must answer in writing before accepting.
- For each of the six areas: NO ISSUE IDENTIFIED, REVIEW NEEDED, or LIKELY DISQUALIFYING, with the one-line reason.
- A recommended alternative arrangement for every REVIEW NEEDED or LIKELY DISQUALIFYING item.
- The exact wording for a file note recording this check, whatever the outcome.
Why this phrasingSplitting perceived conflict into the complainant's view and the respondent's view separately catches the case that looks clean from one side and indefensible from the other.
What could go wrongTreating the output as a clearance. It is a question list. The file note recording that you asked the questions is the artifact that matters, not the model's verdict.
04

Scope definition

Writes the scope statement that goes at the front of the plan and the front of the report. Scope creep is the most common reason an investigation runs long and lands soft.

Prompt 04
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Draft a written scope of investigation.

INPUT
- Framed allegations: [PASTE THE NUMBERED LIST FROM PROMPT 02]
- Who commissioned the investigation and their role: [DESCRIBE]
- Applicable policy or procedure governing investigations: [NAME]
- Known constraints: [TIME, ACCESS, JURISDICTION, PARALLEL PROCESSES]

TASK
Draft a scope statement containing:
1. The allegations in scope, by number, quoted exactly as framed.
2. What is expressly OUT of scope, stated positively. Include anything raised that will be handled through a different route, and name that route.
3. The standard of proof to be applied, and who set it.
4. The decision the investigation will and will not make. State explicitly whether the investigator determines findings of fact only, or also recommends an outcome.
5. Who receives the report.
6. What happens if new allegations surface mid-investigation: the named process for varying scope in writing rather than absorbing them silently.
7. Confidentiality expectations, framed accurately: what will be shared, with whom, and the limits of any assurance.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not write a confidentiality assurance the process cannot keep.
- Do not commit to a completion date the constraints above do not support. If a date is required, express it as a target with the named review point.
- Flag any allegation that a reasonable reader would expect to be in scope and that this scope excludes, and require an explicit reason.

OUTPUT
The scope statement, ready to be edited and signed, plus a short list of decisions the commissioning manager must confirm before it is final.
Why this phrasingStating what is out of scope positively, with the route it goes to instead, is what stops the finished report from looking like the investigator ignored something.
What could go wrongA model will happily insert a two week target because reports usually have one. An unmet date in a signed scope statement becomes evidence of an unfair process. Use a target plus a review point.
05

Evidence preservation notice

The message that goes out early to stop relevant material from being deleted in the ordinary course. Short, specific, and not an accusation.

Prompt 05
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Draft a preservation notice.

CONTEXT
- Recipients and their roles: [LIST BY ROLE]
- Categories of material likely to be relevant: [E.G. EMAIL, CHAT, SHIFT ROTAS, CCTV, DOOR ACCESS LOGS, CASE NOTES, CALENDAR, RECORDINGS]
- Relevant date range: [FROM] to [TO]
- Systems with automatic deletion or short retention: [LIST, WITH RETENTION PERIOD IF KNOWN]
- Jurisdiction: [COUNTRY / STATE]

TASK
Draft two versions of a preservation notice: one for systems and IT custodians, one for individual employees who hold relevant material.

BOTH MUST
- Name the categories and the date range specifically. Never say "anything relevant".
- Instruct recipients to suspend routine deletion for the named categories, and to take no other action.
- State that receiving the notice is not an accusation and implies nothing about any person.
- Give a named contact and a response deadline for confirming preservation is in place.
- Ask the recipient to reply confirming what they have preserved and to flag anything already deleted, with the date.

MUST NOT
- Describe the allegations.
- Name the complainant or the respondent.
- Instruct anyone to review, collect, forward or interpret the material.

ALSO OUTPUT
- The priority order in which to send these, based on which systems delete soonest.
- A one-line file note recording when each notice went out and to whom.
Why this phrasing"Suspend deletion and take no other action" prevents a well-meaning custodian from collecting, summarising, or worse, tidying the material before you see it.
What could go wrongSending it late. CCTV and door logs often roll off in days. Order the sends by retention period, not by seniority.
06

Interim measures decision

The decision you have to make in the first hours and defend for months. Interim measures must protect without pre-judging, and the distinction has to be visible on the page.

Prompt 06
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator considering interim measures during an open investigation. You do not decide. You produce options with the trade-offs made explicit.

CONTEXT
- Allegations, by number and category: [PASTE]
- Working relationship between the parties: [SAME TEAM, REPORTING LINE, SHARED SHIFT, NO CONTACT, ETC.]
- Any stated safety concern: [DESCRIBE OR "NONE STATED"]
- Business constraints: [COVERAGE, CUSTOMER IMPACT, ROTA, LOCATION]
- Applicable policy on suspension or reassignment: [SUMMARISE]

TASK
Produce a range of interim measures from least to most restrictive. For each option give: what it does, who it restricts, how it would be perceived by each party, the business cost, and the risk if it is not taken.

REQUIRED IN EVERY OPTION SET
- At least two options less restrictive than suspension.
- For any option restricting the respondent, the exact neutral wording to use with the wider team, which must not disclose the allegations or imply a conclusion.
- The review interval at which the measure must be reconsidered, and who reviews it.
- What the measure does NOT mean, written to be said out loud to the affected person.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not recommend a single option. Present the set.
- Flag any option that alters pay, status, hours or duties, and mark it "confirm contractual position with counsel".
- Flag any option that would isolate the complainant rather than the respondent as carrying a retaliation risk, and say so plainly.

OUTPUT
The option set, then a template file note recording the decision taken, the reason, the alternatives considered and rejected, and the review date.
Why this phrasingRequiring two options less restrictive than suspension forces the record to show that suspension was a considered choice, not a reflex. That record is what a tribunal asks for.
What could go wrongMeasures that quietly move the complainant. It feels operationally easier and it looks like retaliation on paper. The prompt flags it because the flag is easy to miss under time pressure.
07

Investigation plan generator

Builds the plan that fills Template 2. A written plan made before the first interview is the difference between an investigation and a series of conversations.

Prompt 07
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Draft an investigation plan.

INPUT
- Scope statement: [PASTE FROM PROMPT 04]
- Framed allegations: [PASTE FROM PROMPT 02]
- Known documents and systems: [LIST]
- Known potential witnesses, by role label: [LIST]
- Constraints: [TIME, ACCESS, LEAVE, SHIFT PATTERNS, PARALLEL PROCESSES]

TASK
For each numbered allegation, produce a plan row containing:
1. What must be established for this allegation to be proved on the applicable standard.
2. Evidence that would tend to prove it.
3. Evidence that would tend to DISPROVE it. This row is mandatory and may not be left empty.
4. Where each item of evidence sits, and who holds it.
5. Which witness can speak to it, and what specifically only that person can address.
6. The order of steps, with the reason for the order.

ALSO PRODUCE
- A single consolidated sequence across all allegations, deduplicated, so no witness is interviewed twice for want of planning.
- The points at which the plan should be revisited.
- Anything you cannot plan because the input is missing, listed as an open question rather than assumed.

CONSTRAINTS
- Never assume a document exists. Mark each as CONFIRMED EXISTS or TO BE CONFIRMED.
- Do not schedule the respondent interview before the evidence that will be put to them has been gathered, and say why in one line.
- Keep the plan to what the scope covers. Flag anything you would want to pursue that falls outside scope, and route it to the scope variation process rather than into the plan.

OUTPUT
A plan table, the consolidated sequence, the review points, and the open questions.
Why this phrasingMaking the disproving-evidence row mandatory is the whole point. An investigation that only collects confirming evidence reaches the same conclusion it started with, and it reads that way.
What could go wrongThe model will list documents it assumes any employer has. Confirm each one exists before the plan hardens, or the report will reference a record nobody ever held.
08

Witness list builder

Decides who to interview and, just as importantly, records why you did not interview someone. The second half is what gets challenged.

Prompt 08
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator building a witness list.

INPUT
- Framed allegations: [PASTE]
- People named or implied in the narrative, by role label, with how they came up: [LIST]
- People suggested by the complainant: [LIST]
- People suggested by the respondent: [LIST OR "NOT YET ASKED"]
- Organisational context: team structure, shift patterns, who was physically or virtually present: [DESCRIBE]

TASK
Produce a witness table with one row per person:
- Role label.
- Which numbered allegations they can speak to.
- What specifically they are expected to have direct knowledge of. Distinguish DIRECT (saw or heard it), CONTEXT (background, patterns, working relationships) and HEARSAY (told about it afterwards).
- Priority: essential, useful, or not required.
- Risk factors: reporting line to either party, prior complaint history, likely reluctance, welfare considerations.

ALSO PRODUCE
- A NOT INTERVIEWED list: everyone named or suggested who you are recommending not to interview, with the reason stated in a way that would read fairly to the person who suggested them.
- Anyone who should be interviewed who was suggested by neither party, and why.
- Where the account rests on a single uncorroborated witness, flag it and name what additional evidence could corroborate or undercut it.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not treat a suggestion from either party as automatically in or out.
- Do not recommend interviewing someone solely to characterise a party's general reputation. Flag any such suggestion and say why reputation evidence is a risk.

OUTPUT
The witness table, the not-interviewed list with reasons, and the corroboration flags.
Why this phrasingSplitting direct from context from hearsay early stops a report from resting weight on someone who only heard about it in the kitchen.
What could go wrongQuietly dropping a witness the complainant named. If they are not interviewed, the reason has to survive being read back to the person who named them.
09

Interview order logic

Sequencing is evidence handling. The order you interview in determines what you can put to whom, and whether accounts had a chance to align.

Prompt 09
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator sequencing interviews.

INPUT
- Witness table: [PASTE FROM PROMPT 08]
- Framed allegations: [PASTE]
- Documentary evidence already obtained: [LIST]
- Documentary evidence still outstanding: [LIST]
- Availability and constraints: [LEAVE, SHIFTS, NOTICE PERIODS, REPRESENTATION]

TASK
Produce a recommended interview order. For each position in the order, state:
- Who.
- Why they sit here rather than earlier or later.
- What must be in hand before this interview happens.
- What this interview is expected to open up for later interviews.

APPLY THESE PRINCIPLES AND SAY WHERE EACH ONE BINDS
- The complainant is interviewed first, before their account can be shaped by others.
- The respondent is interviewed after the evidence to be put to them exists, so it can be put fairly and in full.
- Witnesses who work closely together are scheduled to reduce the opportunity for accounts to be compared, and note where that is not achievable.
- Anyone who may become a respondent is not interviewed as a witness. Flag the risk and the point at which status must be reviewed.

ALSO PRODUCE
- The re-interview triggers: what an interview would have to reveal for an earlier interviewee to be revisited.
- A contingency order if a key person is unavailable, so the case does not stall.
- The exact neutral wording for the interview invitation, with the notice period and the representation entitlement left as bracketed inputs for the investigator to complete from policy.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not draft anything that instructs witnesses not to speak to each other beyond what the policy in force permits. Flag it for confirmation instead.
Why this phrasingAsking the model to say where each principle binds turns a generic ordering rule into a reasoned sequence you can defend line by line.
What could go wrongConfidentiality instructions to witnesses. What you may lawfully ask varies by jurisdiction and by whether the discussion is protected activity. That is why the prompt refuses to draft it.
10

Evidence map

Ties every allegation to the evidence that touches it, and exposes the allegations resting on nothing. Feeds Template 4 and the findings report.

Prompt 10
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator building an evidence map.

INPUT
- Framed allegations, numbered: [PASTE]
- Evidence items gathered so far, each with: item reference, type, date, source, how obtained: [LIST]
- Interview accounts obtained so far, by role label: [SUMMARISE, ROLE LABELS ONLY]

TASK
Build a map linking evidence to allegations.

FOR EACH ALLEGATION
- List every evidence item that touches it, by reference number.
- For each item state whether it TENDS TO SUPPORT, TENDS TO UNDERMINE, or is NEUTRAL AND CONTEXTUAL. Do not weigh them against each other yet.
- Identify direct evidence separately from circumstantial evidence.
- State plainly whether the allegation currently rests on: documentary evidence, corroborated account, single uncorroborated account, or nothing yet.

THEN PRODUCE
- Gaps: for each allegation, the specific evidence that does not yet exist and could be obtained, and how.
- Conflicts: every point where two items or two accounts cannot both be accurate, stated as a pair without resolving it.
- Chain of custody flags: any item whose origin, date or completeness cannot be established from the input.
- Items that appear relevant to no allegation in scope, listed separately rather than discarded.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not resolve conflicts. Do not assess credibility. Do not conclude.
- Do not infer the existence of evidence. If an account refers to a document not in the list, record it as a gap.

OUTPUT
The map per allegation, then the gaps, conflicts, custody flags and out-of-scope items.
Why this phrasingRefusing to weigh evidence at map stage keeps the analysis honest. Weighing happens once, in the findings, where the reasoning is visible and reviewable.
What could go wrongThe map reads tidier than reality. An allegation marked "nothing yet" is a real result and must survive into the report, not get quietly padded with context evidence.
11

Complainant interview question bank

The first substantive interview. Aim is a complete account in the complainant's own words, captured before anyone else's version exists.

Prompt 11
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator preparing to interview a complainant. Produce a question bank, not a script to be read verbatim.

INPUT
- Framed allegations: [PASTE]
- What the complainant has already stated at intake: [SUMMARISE]
- Evidence already held that relates to their account: [LIST BY REFERENCE]
- Applicable policy definitions that may be engaged: [PASTE RELEVANT CLAUSES]

TASK
Produce questions grouped by allegation, and within each group ordered from open to specific.

REQUIRED COVERAGE PER ALLEGATION
- A free narrative invitation before any specific question.
- Time, place, who was present, what was said or done, in whose words.
- What happened immediately before and immediately after.
- Whether anything was written down, sent, recorded or photographed at the time, and where it is now.
- Who they told, when, and what they said.
- Effect on them, asked without suggesting a particular effect.
- Whether this was a single event or part of a pattern, and if a pattern, the specific other instances.

ALSO INCLUDE
- Questions that test the account rather than only collect it, phrased neutrally. Testing is not hostility.
- Questions covering anything in the evidence that appears inconsistent with their account, phrased as an invitation to comment rather than a challenge.
- Questions about what outcome they are seeking, and what they would consider resolution.
- A closing block: anything else they want on the record, anything they have not been asked about, the no-retaliation statement, what happens next in process terms only.

CONSTRAINTS
- No question may contain the answer.
- No compound questions. One fact per question.
- No question about why they did not report earlier. Ask what has changed, and what if anything prevented an earlier report.
- Do not include any question about a protected characteristic unless it is squarely engaged by a framed allegation. Where it is, flag it "confirm with counsel".

OUTPUT
The question bank grouped by allegation, then the closing block, then a short list of the evidence items you would have in the room and why.
Why this phrasing"Testing is not hostility" is written into the prompt because models otherwise soften every probing question into an agreeing one, and an untested account is not evidence, it is a statement.
What could go wrongEffect questions that suggest the effect. "How distressing was that" plants the answer. Watch for it and rewrite to "what effect, if any, did that have on you".
12

Respondent interview question bank

The fairness pivot of the whole process. Everything that could count against the respondent has to be put to them, in enough detail to answer.

Prompt 12
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator preparing to interview a respondent. Produce a question bank.

INPUT
- Framed allegations: [PASTE]
- Every evidence item and account that will be put to the respondent, by reference: [LIST]
- What has already been disclosed to the respondent, and when: [DESCRIBE]
- Applicable policy and procedure, including representation entitlement: [SUMMARISE]

TASK
Produce a question bank that puts every allegation and every piece of adverse evidence to the respondent fairly.

MANDATORY STRUCTURE
1. Confirm the respondent understands each allegation as framed, in plain words, before any question about it.
2. Free narrative invitation per allegation, before specifics.
3. Every adverse item put explicitly: what it is, what it appears to show, and an open invitation to respond.
4. Questions inviting evidence in their favour: who else was present, what records exist, what would show a different picture.
5. Questions about context the respondent may raise: instruction from above, custom and practice, prior tolerance of the same conduct, disparity of treatment.
6. Closing block: anything not asked, anything to add, anyone else who should be spoken to, and what they want considered.

CONSTRAINTS
- No allegation may be omitted, softened, or merged with another. Each is put on its own.
- No question may assume the allegation is proved. Compare: "why did you shout at them" versus "the account is that you raised your voice on 4 March. What is your response to that".
- Do not ask the respondent to explain someone else's state of mind.
- Do not include a question that invites the respondent to comment on the complainant's character or credibility generally. Flag any such request and say why it is a risk.
- Flag anywhere the input suggests the respondent has not had enough notice or detail to answer, and stop rather than working around it.

OUTPUT
The question bank, then a completeness check: a list of every allegation and adverse item mapped to the question that puts it, with any gaps named.
Why this phrasingThe completeness check at the end is the deliverable. An allegation that never got put to the respondent is the single most common reason a finding is overturned on appeal.
What could go wrongQuestions written from the complainant's account as if it were established. Reread every question asking whether it presumes the answer, because the model's default register is narrative, not neutral.
13

Witness interview question bank

Witnesses are the easiest interviews to do badly. The failure mode is inviting opinion instead of observation.

Prompt 13
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator preparing to interview a witness.

INPUT
- Witness role label and what they are expected to speak to: [DESCRIBE]
- Their classification from the witness table: DIRECT / CONTEXT / HEARSAY
- The specific allegations they touch, by number: [LIST]
- Their reporting relationship to either party: [DESCRIBE]

TASK
Produce a question bank appropriate to their classification.

FOR ALL WITNESSES
- Opening: their role, working relationship to the parties, and how they came to be identified.
- Free narrative of what they observed, before any specific question.
- Sensory grounding: what they personally saw, personally heard, where they were standing or sitting, what else was happening, how clearly they could see or hear.
- Distinguish, in every answer, what they observed from what they were told and from what they concluded. Include an explicit question separating the three.
- Contemporaneous records: notes, messages, calendar entries, anything sent at the time.
- Whether they have discussed the matter with either party or with other witnesses, when, and what was said.

ADDITIONAL FOR CONTEXT WITNESSES
- Pattern questions tied to specific, dated instances only. Never general impressions of a person.

ADDITIONAL FOR HEARSAY WITNESSES
- Who told them, when, in what words, and whether anything was recorded at the time. Mark the entire account as second-hand at the top of the record.

CONSTRAINTS
- No question invites an opinion on whether an allegation is true.
- No question invites a general character assessment of either party.
- No question reveals more of the allegations than the witness needs to answer.
- Include the standard opening and closing scripts: purpose, confidentiality as the policy actually permits, no retaliation, and the right to add anything later.

OUTPUT
The question bank, the opening and closing scripts, and a note of what this witness should NOT be told.
Why this phrasingSensory grounding questions are the reason a witness account survives challenge. "Where were you standing" and "what else was happening" produce a testable account instead of a remembered impression.
What could go wrongOver-disclosure. Telling a witness the allegation to get a better answer contaminates it. The "should NOT be told" list exists to be read before you walk in.
14

Open to closed sequencing

A rewriting pass over any question set you already have. Fixes the leading, compound and closed-too-early questions that survive a first draft.

Prompt 14
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Audit and re-sequence a set of interview questions. Do not add new subject matter.

INPUT
Question set to audit: [PASTE]
Interview type: complainant / respondent / witness
Purpose of this interview in one line: [STATE]

TASK
For every question, do three things:
1. Classify it: OPEN, PROBING, CLOSED, LEADING, COMPOUND, or ASSUMPTIVE.
2. Where it is LEADING, COMPOUND or ASSUMPTIVE, rewrite it. Show the original and the rewrite side by side so the investigator can see what changed.
3. Place it in the correct position in a funnel: free narrative first, then open questions, then probing questions, then closed questions to pin specifics, then confirmation.

THEN
- Flag any topic that jumps to a closed question with no open question before it, and supply the missing open question.
- Flag any question that could be answered yes or no where a yes or no answer would be useless, and rewrite it.
- Flag any place where the sequence would signal to the interviewee what answer is expected next.
- Produce the final sequenced set, numbered, with the funnel stage marked against each question.

CONSTRAINTS
- Preserve the substance. Every topic in the input must survive to the output.
- Do not merge questions to shorten the set. Splitting is allowed, merging is not.
- Keep the total answerable in the time available: [MINUTES]. If it will not fit, do not cut topics, mark the lowest priority questions as reserve.

OUTPUT
The classification table with rewrites, the flags, and the final numbered sequence.
Why this phrasingShowing the original next to the rewrite is what teaches the pattern. After three cases you will stop writing leading questions and this prompt becomes a check rather than a fix.
What could go wrongLetting it merge questions to hit a time budget. Merging creates compound questions, which is the exact defect the prompt is meant to remove.

Credibility assessment framing

The most dangerous analysis in an investigation, and the one most often done on instinct. This prompt forces the reasoning onto the page where it can be checked.

Prompt 15
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator with a credibility framework. You do NOT assess credibility. You produce the framework and the questions the investigator must answer in writing.

INPUT
- The competing accounts, by role label, on the specific point in dispute: [PASTE]
- Evidence touching that point, by reference: [LIST]
- The specific factual question in dispute, stated in one sentence: [STATE]

TASK
Produce a structured framework for the investigator to complete.

FOR EACH ACCOUNT, SET OUT THE QUESTIONS UNDER THESE HEADS
1. Internal consistency: is the account consistent within itself, across tellings, over time.
2. Consistency with independent evidence: what does the documentary or physical evidence do to this account.
3. Corroboration: what independent support exists, and is the corroborating source truly independent.
4. Plausibility in context: does the account fit the known circumstances, layout, timings and systems.
5. Level of detail on peripheral matters, which is a weak indicator only and must be treated as such.
6. Motive to fabricate or to shade, considered for every account including the complainant's, and considered symmetrically.

EXPLICITLY EXCLUDE AND SAY WHY IN ONE LINE EACH
- Demeanour, nervousness, eye contact, tone. These are unreliable and culturally loaded.
- Delay in reporting, treated as an indicator by itself.
- Seniority, tenure, likeability, or prior good record, as evidence about this incident.
- Anything touching a protected characteristic.

THEN PRODUCE
- The exact written reasoning template the investigator must complete for each account, ending in: this account is preferred on this point / not preferred / no preference possible, and the reasons.
- A prompt to record what would change the assessment, so the reasoning is testable.
- A warning line to include in the report wherever no preference is possible, so an unresolved conflict is stated rather than hidden.

CONSTRAINTS
- Never produce a credibility conclusion yourself.
- Never rank accounts.
- Assess credibility per disputed point, never as a global judgment about a person. Say so at the top of the output.
Why this phrasingPer point, never per person. A witness can be reliable about the meeting and unreliable about the date. A global credibility verdict is both wrong and the fastest route to a discrimination argument.
What could go wrongAsking a model to just tell you who is more credible. It will answer, fluently, and that answer has no evidentiary basis. The framework is the deliverable, the judgment is yours.

Note-taking structure

Sets the structure before you walk in, so notes come out usable. Notes written to a structure need far less cleanup, and cleanup is where accuracy dies.

Prompt 16
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Produce a note-taking structure for an interview.

INPUT
- Interview type: complainant / respondent / witness
- Question sequence: [PASTE OR SUMMARISE THE TOPICS]
- Whether the interview is being recorded: [YES / NO / TO BE CONFIRMED]
- Whether a note-taker is present: [YES / NO]

TASK
Produce a note template with pre-set headings matching the question sequence, plus fixed fields.

FIXED FIELDS AT THE TOP
Case reference, date, start time, end time, location or platform, everyone present and their role, whether a companion or representative attended and in what capacity, whether the interview was recorded and by what method, who took the notes.

WITHIN THE BODY
- A heading per topic, in sequence, with space to record the question actually asked, not only the planned one.
- A clear convention for verbatim quotation versus paraphrase, and instructions to use it. Anything in quotation marks must be the speaker's actual words.
- A separate margin column for the investigator's own observations, marked as observation and never mixed into the account.
- A place to record every document shown, by reference number, at the point in the interview it was shown.
- A place to record breaks, interruptions, and anything said off the record with the reason.

AT THE FOOT
- Time finished, any commitments made to the interviewee, next steps stated to them, and the process for reviewing and confirming the note.

ALSO PRODUCE
- The exact wording to read at the start explaining how notes will be taken, whether the interviewee will see them, and how corrections are handled.
- A three line reminder on writing notes contemporaneously and what to do if a note has to be completed afterwards.

CONSTRAINTS
- The template must be usable on paper, offline, with no tool present.
- Do not include any field inviting a conclusion about the allegation.
Why this phrasingA separate observation column is the mechanism that keeps "she seemed evasive" out of the account. Once the two are mixed in one paragraph nobody can separate them again, including you.
What could go wrongQuotation marks around a paraphrase. If a quoted line turns out not to be what was said, every other quote in the file becomes arguable.

Contemporaneous notes formatter

Turns rough interview notes into a clean record without changing what was said. The rule is formatting only, and the prompt enforces it.

Prompt 17
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Format rough interview notes into a clean record. You are a formatter, not an editor.

INPUT
Rough notes, role labels only: [PASTE]
Interview metadata: [CASE REF, DATE, TIMES, PRESENT, RECORDED YES/NO]

ABSOLUTE RULES
- Do not add any fact, name, date, time, place or detail that is not in the input.
- Do not smooth, complete or interpret an incomplete answer. Reproduce it as incomplete.
- Do not convert paraphrase into quotation. Only text already marked as verbatim may appear in quotation marks.
- Do not reorder the substance of what was said. You may group under headings, but the sequence within each topic stays as recorded.
- Do not remove anything, including material that seems irrelevant or unhelpful.
- Do not resolve a contradiction inside the notes. Reproduce both parts and flag the pair.

TASK
1. Apply the metadata header.
2. Organise the content under the topic headings present in the notes.
3. Mark clearly, using distinct labels: ACCOUNT (what the interviewee said), QUESTION (what was asked), OBSERVATION (the note-taker's own words), DOCUMENT SHOWN (with reference), and ADMIN (breaks, interruptions).
4. Where the notes are illegible, ambiguous, truncated or abbreviated beyond certainty, insert [UNCLEAR IN ORIGINAL] rather than guessing.
5. Produce a GAPS list: every point where the record is incomplete and would need the interviewee to confirm.
6. Produce a CHANGES list: every formatting change you made, so the investigator can compare against the original.

OUTPUT
The formatted record, the gaps list, the changes list, and a one-line statement of what you did not do.
Why this phrasingThe changes list is the audit trail. Without it, a formatted note is indistinguishable from an altered note, and the original stops being able to defend the copy.
What could go wrongSilent completion. A model asked to tidy notes will finish a half-written sentence because that reads better. Keep the original file, always, and compare.
18

Finding of fact, separating fact from opinion

The prompt that does the most work in the pack. Everything that reads as a finding but is actually an inference gets pulled apart here.

Prompt 18
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator. Separate fact from inference from opinion in draft findings. You do not decide whether anything is proved.

INPUT
Draft findings text: [PASTE]
Evidence references available: [LIST]

TASK
Take every statement in the input and classify it into exactly one of four categories.

1. FACT SUPPORTED BY EVIDENCE. A statement about what happened that can be traced to a named evidence reference. Rewrite it in the form: [WHAT HAPPENED], on [DATE], evidenced by [REFERENCE].
2. FACT ASSERTED WITHOUT EVIDENCE. Reads as fact, traces to nothing in the list. Rewrite it as an open question and name the evidence that would settle it.
3. INFERENCE. A conclusion drawn from facts. Rewrite it to show its structure: because [FACT, REFERENCE] and [FACT, REFERENCE], it is more likely than not that [INFERENCE]. Then state what alternative explanation was considered and why it is less likely.
4. OPINION OR CHARACTERISATION. A judgment about a person, their motives, their attitude or their character. Either delete it, or rewrite it as the observable conduct underneath it plus its evidence reference.

FLAG SEPARATELY
- Every adjective doing evidentiary work: aggressive, hostile, unprofessional, inappropriate, dismissive, erratic. Replace each with the conduct described.
- Every statement about what a person knew, intended, meant or felt, unless they said so and it is referenced.
- Every use of "clearly", "obviously", "evidently", "of course". These signal an unstated inference.
- Every passive construction that hides who did what.
- Every generalisation across incidents that is not itself supported.

OUTPUT
The four classified lists with rewrites, the flag list, and a clean redraft containing only category 1 and category 3, with every category 3 item showing its reasoning.
Why this phrasingRequiring every inference to show its structure, and to name the alternative explanation it rejected, is what makes a finding reviewable. A conclusion nobody can trace backwards is not a finding, it is an assertion.
What could go wrongAdjectives sneaking back in during the final polish. Run this prompt again on the finished report, not only on the draft.
19

Standard of proof application

Applies the balance of probabilities to each allegation explicitly, and forces the "not proved" answer to be as well reasoned as the "proved" one.

Prompt 19
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator applying a standard of proof. You produce the reasoning structure. The investigator makes the determination.

INPUT
- Standard of proof that applies, and its source: [E.G. BALANCE OF PROBABILITIES, PER POLICY X]
- Allegation, stated exactly as framed: [PASTE ONE ALLEGATION]
- Evidence tending to support, by reference: [LIST]
- Evidence tending to undermine, by reference: [LIST]
- Points of conflict and how each was resolved, or that it was not resolved: [PASTE]

TASK
Produce the written reasoning for this single allegation.

STRUCTURE
1. Restate the allegation and the specific factual question that must be answered.
2. State the standard being applied in plain words, and say what it does not mean. Balance of probabilities means more likely than not. It does not mean beyond reasonable doubt, and it does not mean the account is beyond challenge.
3. Set out the supporting evidence with its weight and its limits.
4. Set out the undermining evidence with its weight and its limits. This section may not be shorter than section 3 without an explicit reason.
5. Address every unresolved conflict. An unresolved conflict is stated, not omitted.
6. State the conclusion in one of exactly three forms: PROVED on the standard applied / NOT PROVED on the standard applied / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE TO DETERMINE, and give the reasons in full sentences.
7. Where NOT PROVED, state explicitly that this means the allegation was not established on the evidence available, and that it is not a finding that the complainant was untruthful. This sentence is mandatory.

CONSTRAINTS
- Never state a conclusion the input does not support.
- Never treat the absence of corroboration as evidence that the allegation is false. Address it as a limit on weight.
- Never import a criminal standard. Flag any language that implies one.
- Repeat this prompt once per allegation. Do not batch allegations into a single reasoning block.

OUTPUT
The reasoning for this allegation only, ready for the report.
Why this phrasingThe mandatory "not proved does not mean untruthful" sentence protects the complainant from the most damaging misreading of an investigation report, and it protects the employer from the retaliation claim that follows the misreading.
What could go wrongBatching allegations. Reasoning written once across five allegations lets a strong one carry a weak one, and that is visible to anyone reading carefully.

Protected characteristic and disparity language check

A review pass over anything before it leaves your hands. Catches the language that turns a clean investigation into a discrimination exhibit.

Prompt 20
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator reviewing draft investigation text for language risk. You do not give legal advice. You flag for human and legal review.

INPUT
Text to review: [PASTE]
Jurisdiction: [COUNTRY / STATE]
Document type: notes / findings / report / letter

TASK
Flag every instance in these categories. Quote the exact line, say why it is a risk, and offer a neutral rewrite.

1. Direct reference to a protected characteristic where it is not squarely engaged by an allegation.
2. Proxy language that stands in for a protected characteristic: cultural fit, professionalism applied to appearance or speech, articulate, aggressive, emotional, difficult, abrasive, not a team player, communication style, attitude.
3. Stereotype-adjacent framing, including assumptions about caring responsibilities, stamina, ambition, language ability, or religious observance.
4. Age proxies: energy, digital native, overqualified, set in their ways, nearing retirement.
5. Disability and health proxies: reliability where absence is the underlying issue, unpredictable, high maintenance, and any reference to a health condition not disclosed for this process.
6. Disparity of treatment: any place where the text applies a standard to one person that the evidence shows was not applied to a comparator. Name the comparator and the inconsistency.
7. Retaliation exposure: any language that treats raising a complaint, seeking representation, taking leave, or contacting a regulator as a negative.
8. Conclusions about a person's character rather than their conduct.

THEN PRODUCE
- A prioritised list: MUST CHANGE, SHOULD CHANGE, CONSIDER.
- Everything requiring employment counsel review before the document leaves the investigator, listed separately with the reason.
- A short statement of what you did NOT review, so the reader does not mistake this for a legal sign-off.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not remove substance. Flag and propose, never silently rewrite the meaning.
- Where a protected characteristic IS squarely engaged, say so and do not flag it as a problem. Absence of the topic is not neutrality.
Why this phrasingProxy language is the real exposure. Nobody writes the protected characteristic. They write "abrasive" and "not a culture fit", and those words sit in the file next to a pattern.
What could go wrongReading the output as legal clearance. It is a flag list with a stated scope limit. The counsel review line at the end exists so the file records what was and was not checked.

Findings and report drafting

The centrepiece. Drafts the report that fills Template 5, structured so a reader who was not there can follow the reasoning from allegation to conclusion.

Prompt 21
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator drafting an investigation report. You draft. The investigator determines every finding and owns every word that ships.

INPUT
- Scope statement: [PASTE]
- Framed allegations, numbered: [PASTE]
- Evidence schedule, each item with reference, type, date, source, how obtained: [PASTE]
- Interview records, by role label: [PASTE OR SUMMARISE]
- Per-allegation reasoning from Prompt 19: [PASTE]
- Standard of proof and its source: [STATE]
- Who the report is for and what decision they will make with it: [STATE]

TASK
Draft the report in this structure, and no other.

1. Cover: case reference, investigator, date commissioned, date of report, standard of proof applied, distribution list.
2. Executive summary: allegations and outcomes only, one line each, no reasoning, no recommendations.
3. Scope: in scope, out of scope with the route taken instead, and any variation to scope with the date and who approved it.
4. Process: what was done, in date order. Who was interviewed and when, what documents were obtained and from where, what was requested and not obtained and why, what steps were taken to be fair to the respondent.

6. Findings, one section per allegation, each containing: the allegation as framed, the evidence for, the evidence against, the conflicts and how they were resolved or that they were not, the reasoning, and the conclusion in the exact three-form language from Prompt 19.

- The executive summary contains nothing that does not appear in full in the body.
- Where the evidence does not support a conclusion, say so in that form. Do not soften it into a suggestion.
- Add nothing not present in the input. Where the input is missing something the structure requires, insert [INVESTIGATOR TO COMPLETE: WHAT IS NEEDED].

OUTPUT
The full draft, then a completeness check listing: allegations with no conclusion, statements with no evidence reference, and every [INVESTIGATOR TO COMPLETE] marker.
Why this phrasingSeparating the executive summary from the reasoning, and banning reasoning from the summary, stops the one section most people read from becoming the version of record.
What could go wrongRecommendations appearing uninvited. A report that recommends dismissal when the scope asked only for findings has made the decision-maker's decision for them, and a dismissal made that way is challengeable on process alone.

Outcome determination framing

For when the scope does put the outcome decision in your hands. Structures consistency and mitigation so the decision is defensible against the next comparable case.

Prompt 22
You are assisting a decision-maker considering an outcome after findings. You produce the framework and the questions. You do not recommend an outcome.

INPUT
- Findings, by allegation, with conclusions: [PASTE]
- Applicable policy, including the range of sanctions available and any defined categories: [PASTE]
- Comparable prior cases in this organisation, anonymised, with what happened and the outcome: [LIST OR "NONE IDENTIFIED"]
- Respondent's record: length of service, prior live warnings, prior findings: [STATE]
- Mitigation advanced by the respondent: [PASTE]
- Any regulatory, licensing or safety dimension: [STATE OR "NONE"]

TASK
Produce the decision framework.

1. Which specific findings, by number, are capable of supporting an outcome, and which are not. A NOT PROVED finding may not support any outcome and must be stated as excluded.
2. The policy category each proved finding falls into, quoted from the policy, with the question that determines the category where it is arguable.
3. The consistency check: for each comparable case listed, what is materially the same and what is materially different. Where a different outcome would result, the difference must be identifiable from the findings, not from the people involved.
4. Aggravating factors, each tied to a specific finding.
5. Mitigating factors, including everything the respondent advanced, each addressed rather than listed. An unaddressed mitigation is a gap.
6. The range of outcomes available on this policy, from lightest to heaviest, with what each would require the decision-maker to have concluded.
7. Proportionality questions the decision-maker must answer in writing.
8. Anything requiring counsel or a regulator before the outcome is issued.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not select an outcome.
- Do not import an outcome from the comparable cases. Comparators inform consistency, they do not decide.
- Flag any factor that is a proxy for a protected characteristic or for the exercise of a right, and exclude it explicitly.
- Where the record contains no comparables, say that consistency cannot be evidenced and name what should be recorded now so the next case has one.

OUTPUT
The framework, then the written record template the decision-maker completes, ending with the outcome, the reasons, and the appeal route.
Why this phrasingRequiring each mitigation to be addressed rather than listed is the difference between a decision that considered the person and one that filed their submission. The second is where appeals are won.
What could go wrongLetting an unproved allegation influence the weight of a proved one. The framework excludes them explicitly because in practice they leak in.

Remediation and recommendation

The part almost everyone skips. Most investigations surface a systemic problem, and the file that names it is worth far more than the one that only names a person.

Prompt 23
You are assisting a qualified HR investigator producing remediation recommendations. These are organisational, not disciplinary.

INPUT
- Findings, including NOT PROVED ones: [PASTE]
- Matters arising outside the allegations: [PASTE]
- What the process revealed about how this was allowed to happen or to continue: [DESCRIBE]
- Existing controls that should have caught it: [POLICY, TRAINING, ESCALATION ROUTE, MANAGER CHECK-INS]

TASK
Produce recommendations under four heads.

1. IMMEDIATE, in days. Restoring working relationships, ongoing safety, no-contact arrangements, communication to the affected team.
2. STRUCTURAL, in weeks. Policy gaps, unclear escalation routes, missing records, a manager population that did not know what to do.
3. CULTURAL, ongoing. Patterns the evidence showed, including anything that came up repeatedly and was outside scope.
4. PROCESS, for the next case. What made this investigation harder than it needed to be, and what to record differently from now on.

FOR EACH RECOMMENDATION
- State the specific finding or observation it responds to, by reference.
- Name the owner by role, not by person.
- Give a target date or interval.
- Say how anyone would know later whether it was done.

CONSTRAINTS
- Do not recommend a sanction. That is a different decision under Prompt 22.
- Do not recommend training as a default. Where training is the answer, state what specifically would change as a result. Where it is not, say so.
- Include recommendations that follow from NOT PROVED findings. A complaint that was not established can still have surfaced a real problem, and ignoring that is how the second complaint arrives.
- Flag any recommendation that would in practice disadvantage the complainant, including moving them, changing their duties or altering their reporting line.

OUTPUT
The four grouped lists, then a one-page summary suitable for the person who will be asked to fund or approve them.
Why this phrasingRecommendations that follow from a not-proved finding are the ones that prevent the repeat. Closing a case as unfounded and changing nothing is how the same facts arrive again with a lawyer attached.
What could go wrongDefault training recommendations. "Refresher training for all managers" costs real money, changes nothing measurable, and signals to a reader that the recommendations were not thought through.

Post-closure retaliation check

Scheduled after closure, at set intervals. Retaliation claims frequently outlive and outgrow the original complaint, and they are the most preventable exposure in the whole process.

Prompt 25
You are assisting a qualified HR practitioner running a post-closure retaliation check.

INPUT
- Case reference, closure date, and today's date: [STATE]
- Who raised the complaint and who participated as a witness, by role label: [LIST]
- Outcome issued: [STATE]
- Working arrangements since closure: reporting lines, team, shift, location: [DESCRIBE]
- Anything that has happened since closure involving any participant: [LIST OR "NOTHING REPORTED"]

TASK
Produce a structured check to run at 30, 60 and 90 days after closure.

1. The objective signals to review before speaking to anyone: changes to duties, rota, shift allocation, overtime access, reporting line, location, performance ratings, absence, new performance concerns raised, exclusion from meetings or distribution lists, training or promotion decisions, and any resignation.
2. For each signal, the question that separates a legitimate business reason from a retaliation risk, and what evidence would establish the legitimate reason.
3. A short, non-leading check-in script for the complainant and separately for each witness. It must not reopen the findings, must not invite a new complaint by implication, and must make clear that raising something is welcome.
4. A parallel check on the respondent for signals of unfair informal consequence, because a process that produces informal punishment beyond the outcome is its own exposure.
5. Escalation triggers: what turns a signal into an immediate action, and who acts.

- A file note template recording the check was run, what was reviewed, what was found and what was done, so a completed check with no findings is still evidenced.
- The wording to record a decision NOT to run a check at a given interval, with the reason.

- Never treat the absence of a report as evidence that nothing happened. Review the objective signals regardless.
- Do not draft anything that discourages a participant from raising a further concern.
Why this phrasingReviewing objective signals before speaking to anyone is what catches the quiet retaliation nobody reports. Rota changes and dropped overtime do not generate complaints until they generate a claim.
What could go wrongA check that reopens the case. The script has to acknowledge and route, not relitigate, or the complainant reasonably believes the finding is back in play.

Complainant outcome letter

Says enough to be fair to the complainant without disclosing what cannot be disclosed about another employee. The line between those two is where these letters go wrong.

Prompt 26
You are assisting a qualified HR practitioner drafting an outcome letter to a complainant.

INPUT
- Allegations as framed, numbered: [PASTE]
- Conclusion per allegation: [PASTE]
- What may be disclosed about any action taken regarding another employee, per policy and jurisdiction: [STATE THE LIMIT]
- Appeal or review route available to the complainant, with the deadline: [STATE]
- Support available: [EAP, OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH, UNION, NAMED CONTACT]
- Tone context: how long this has taken and anything the complainant has experienced during the process: [DESCRIBE]

TASK
Draft the letter.

MUST INCLUDE
- Acknowledgement that they raised the complaint and that it was taken seriously, without gratitude framing that reads as management language.
- Each allegation, restated in their terms, with the conclusion reached and a plain explanation of what that conclusion means.
- For any NOT PROVED conclusion, the explicit statement that it means the allegation was not established on the available evidence and is not a finding that they were untruthful.
- A plain-word explanation of the standard of proof applied.
- What has been done, expressed only within the disclosure limit stated above, and an honest statement of what cannot be shared and why.
- The no-retaliation commitment and exactly what to do if they experience anything they believe is retaliation, with a named contact.
- The appeal or review route and the deadline.
- The support available.

MUST NOT
- Disclose any sanction applied to another employee beyond the stated limit.
- Apologise in terms that concede liability. Acknowledge impact without admitting a legal conclusion.
- Use language that implies the complaint was a burden or that the matter is now behind everyone.
- Promise anything about the respondent's future behaviour.
- Exceed one and a half pages.

OUTPUT
The letter, then a checklist confirming each MUST INCLUDE item is present, then a list of anything requiring counsel review before sending.
Why this phrasingBanning gratitude framing sounds small and is not. "Thank you for bringing this to our attention" reads, to someone who has been through a months-long process, exactly like being managed.
What could go wrongOver-disclosure about the respondent. It feels like fairness to the complainant and it is a data protection problem and a defamation risk in the same sentence.

Respondent outcome letter

Has to be precise enough to be appealable. A respondent who cannot tell from the letter what was found and why cannot exercise a right of appeal, and that alone can undo the outcome.

Prompt 27

INPUT
- Allegations as framed, numbered: [PASTE]
- Conclusion per allegation, with the essential reasoning: [PASTE]
- Outcome decided, and by whom, with their role: [STATE]
- Policy provisions relied on: [QUOTE]
- Appeal route, to whom, by when, and on what grounds: [STATE]
- Mitigation advanced and how it was taken into account: [PASTE]

- Support available: [EAP, OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH, UNION, NAMED CONTACT]

Draft the letter.

- The outcome, stated plainly, with the date it takes effect and how long it remains live if it is a warning.
- How the mitigation they advanced was taken into account, addressed rather than acknowledged.
- Any expectations or conditions going forward, stated so that compliance is observable.
- The appeal route, the deadline, who to send it to, and the grounds available.
- The support available, offered without condition.
- The no-retaliation statement covering anyone who participated.

MUST NOT
- Restate an allegation that was not proved as though it were a concern.
- Include commentary on character, attitude or general performance.
- Reference any prior matter that is no longer live under the policy.
- Disclose witness identities beyond what fairness required during the process.
- Be so brief that the respondent cannot identify what was found and why.

OUTPUT
The letter, an appealability check confirming a reader could identify what to appeal and on what basis, and a list of anything requiring counsel review before sending.
Why this phrasingThe appealability check is the whole safeguard. A letter that says "the allegations were upheld and you are issued a final written warning" and stops there is not appealable, and an appeal body will say so.
What could go wrongUnproved allegations reappearing as "concerns". It is the most common defect in these letters and it converts a clean outcome into evidence of a predetermined process.

Manager and team communication

What the people around the case get told. Say too little and the vacuum fills with rumour, say too much and you have disclosed a personnel matter to a room.

Prompt 28

INPUT

- What may be disclosed, per policy and jurisdiction: [STATE THE LIMIT]
- Who needs to know something to do their job, and what specifically: [LIST BY ROLE]
- Practical arrangements that change: [DESCRIBE]
- Whether either party has asked for something specific to be said: [DESCRIBE]

TASK
Produce three separate communications.

1. TO THE LINE MANAGER OF EACH PARTY. Only what they need to manage: practical arrangements, what they must not ask about, what to do if either party raises the matter, what to do if they observe anything that could be retaliation, and who to call. Include an explicit line that they must not investigate further or discuss the matter with the team.
2. TO THE IMMEDIATE TEAM, only if a communication is genuinely needed. Acknowledge only what is already visible, state that a process has concluded without characterising it, state that no further detail will be shared and why, and restate expectations going forward. If no communication is needed, say so and explain why silence is the better option here.
3. HOLDING LINES for predictable questions, with a short answer to each: what happened, is anyone in trouble, is it safe, why can you not tell us, is this why so-and-so is not here, should we be worried.

CONSTRAINTS
- Never confirm or deny an outcome about an individual to a group.
- Never characterise either party.
- Never imply that raising a complaint caused disruption.
- Every line must be sayable out loud by a manager under pressure without improvising. Short sentences.
- Flag anything in the input that has already been over-disclosed, and give the recovery line for it.

OUTPUT
The three communications, then a one-line risk note on each covering the most likely way it gets misread.
Why this phrasingHolding lines have to be sayable under pressure. A manager who has to improvise will say something true, unhelpful and quotable, and it will be attributed to the organisation.
What could go wrongSending a team message when none was needed. The message itself becomes the disclosure, and it tells a room that something happened that they had not all noticed.

No-retaliation reminder

A short standalone communication that goes to everyone who touched the case. Cheap to send, and it is the document that shows the commitment was made in specific terms, not just written in a policy.

Prompt 29
You are assisting a qualified HR practitioner drafting a no-retaliation reminder.

INPUT
- Recipients and their role in the case: complainant, respondent, witnesses, managers of any participant: [LIST BY ROLE]
- Stage: at the start of the investigation / during / at closure: [STATE]
- Applicable policy and any statutory protection that applies in this jurisdiction: [NAME]
- Named contact for concerns, and an alternative route if that contact is part of the problem: [STATE]

TASK
Draft a version for each recipient group. Each must be short enough to be read in full.

EVERY VERSION MUST
- State plainly that retaliation against anyone who raised a concern or participated is not permitted.
- Give concrete examples of what retaliation can look like, so it is recognisable rather than abstract: changes to shifts, duties or reporting line, exclusion from meetings or opportunities, a sudden change in how performance is described, informal freezing out, comments to others.
- State that it applies to participants regardless of the outcome of the investigation, including where allegations were not proved.
- State that it covers indirect action taken through others.
- Give the named contact, the alternative route, and how to raise something confidentially.
- Confirm that raising a retaliation concern is itself protected.

VERSION SPECIFIC
- To managers, add that they must consult before making any change to a participant's duties, rota, reporting line or performance record while the matter is live or recently closed, and name what "recently" means here.
- To the respondent, add the same protection for them and the same expectation of them, stated without implying guilt.
- To witnesses, add that their participation will not be recorded anywhere that affects their standing.

CONSTRAINTS
- Under 250 words per version.
- No legal citations in the body. Reference the policy by name only.
- No language implying anyone is suspected of intending to retaliate.

OUTPUT
Each version, plus a one-line file note recording the reminder was issued, to whom and when.
Why this phrasingConcrete examples do the work. "Retaliation is prohibited" stops nobody, because the manager quietly reallocating overtime does not think that is what they are doing.
What could go wrongSending it once at the start and never again. Retaliation risk is highest right after the outcome lands, which is exactly when most processes stop communicating.

THE RECONSTRUCTION TEST
[ ] A person who was not there can follow every conclusion back to the evidence that produced it

When you hit an edge case

Some cases go outside what any template can carry. Here is how to recognise one, and what to do.

The cases that need a person, not a prompt

  • The respondent is more senior than anyone who could realistically hold them to account.
  • The complaint arrives after a performance decision, or a performance decision arrives after a complaint.
  • Two processes are running at once and each is waiting for the other.
  • The evidence is in a system nobody in HR can access, and the person who can access it is a party.
  • The complainant asks for confidentiality that the investigation cannot honour, and will withdraw without it.
  • Every account conflicts and there is no document anywhere.
  • The organisation has done nothing about the same pattern twice before and the file will show that.
