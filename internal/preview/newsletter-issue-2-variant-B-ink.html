The kit I open before a Monday discovery call

A new prospect asked for a discovery call this past week. Thursday afternoon, calendar invite for Monday morning, no agenda, no signal beyond "let us talk about your situation".

I have a kit for that. Eight prompts, one checklist, one short pre-call script. I open the kit. I paste two references the prospect mentioned into the voice-brief prompt. I get back one paragraph that tells me what their world sounds like in their own words. I write three questions I would not have written without that paragraph.

The call goes 45 minutes. They book a follow-up.
 

What is in the kit, and why each piece earns its place

The eight prompts are not generic. Each one is the smallest version of a thing I do anyway. Read the references. Find the language. Write three questions. Send the recap. None of these are insights I did not already have. The kit is the muscle memory I want to use without thinking about it.

The checklist is six lines. Did I read the references. Did I find one specific quote. Did I write the recap before I close the tab. The list is short because long lists do not get used.

What I almost did not ship

The kit had two extra prompts I removed. They were clever. They asked the LLM to predict objections. I tested them on three real calls. The objections it predicted were not the objections that came up. The cleverness cost me trust in the kit. They came off the shelf.

I keep a small file called off-the-shelf.md with notes on what came out and why. It is the most useful file I have.
