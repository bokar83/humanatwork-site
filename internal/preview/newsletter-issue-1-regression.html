Why I am not selling a course

I have been building an operator stack for myself for the better part of a year. Prompts I paste, kits I open when a client meeting is on the calendar in 20 minutes, small scripts I run when I do not want to think. None of it was packaged. It just sat in my own working memory.

Then a few people asked me what I use. Not in the polite "share your tools" way, in the "I have a meeting in two hours and I am stuck" way. So I started cleaning up the kits one at a time and putting them on a shelf at humanatwork.ai/store.

That shelf is what this newsletter is about. Operator stack, not another course.
 

The difference between a stack and a course

A course teaches you a concept and asks you to apply it later. A stack is the thing you already use, handed to you in the format you already use it. No modules. No watch-time. No "complete the workbook by Friday".

I paste these prompts into my own work. The kits I sell are the same kits I open before a discovery call or a referral ask. If a kit does not survive my own Monday morning, it does not ship.
 

What is on the shelf today

Three kits are live. A founder companion deck for solo consultants going into discovery. A prospect voice brief that turns three references into one personalized opener. A convert-or-kill rubric for landing pages you are not sure about.

More ships every week. Some kits will land. Some will not. The ones that do not earn their place come off the shelf.
